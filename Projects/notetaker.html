        How it works

        Four steps, and you only do the first.

        The calendar is the source of truth. The app keeps "one bot per meeting" true as your calendar changes.

        Connect

        Sign in with Google and allow read-only calendar access. The refresh token goes to Recall.ai, which watches the calendar.

        Book

        Every event with a Meet, Zoom or Teams link in the next 14 days gets a bot. Meetings already in progress get one immediately.

        Record

        At start time the bot asks to join. After someone admits it, it records and transcribes with speaker names.

        Summarise

        GPT-5 mini writes the summary and action items, each checked against the transcript. Then it's emailed and saved.

        Engineering

        The decisions that make it trustworthy.

        Recording people is sensitive, and AI can make things up. Most of the work went into keeping the bot under control and the notes honest.

        Evidence check

        Every action item must quote the exact words where someone made the promise. If the quote isn't in the transcript, the item is dropped before anyone sees it.

        Kick-out = no notes

        If anyone removes the bot from a call, the meeting is marked "Bot removed", the recording is deleted, and nothing is summarised or emailed.

        Never twice

        Webhooks and polling can arrive at the same moment. A single atomic database update decides who summarises, so there's one summary and one email per meeting.

        One bot per meeting

        Bookings use a dedup key of start time + link, so a meeting on several people's calendars gets one shared bot, and each person still gets their own notes.

        Read-only and private

        Calendar access is read-only, the Google token lives with Recall rather than in our database, and every page and API is scoped to the signed-in user.

        Honest about limits

        Meet and Zoom hold bots in a waiting room, so someone clicks Admit once. The docs explain the two ways around it rather than pretending it doesn't exist.

        Stack & cost
